The replacement grows a three-to-five-arrow body cycle from surface walks and sampled contacts, then redirects its opener at a sampled route cell. It installs no actual circle. A separately grown second approach enters the mirror from a different direction. Reflection must release the primary in a reachable state; it must also change the secondary's clear-move ordering. Often the glyph holds that approach against the body cycle until the primary unwinds it.
